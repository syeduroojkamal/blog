### Land Revenue Systems in British India

---

There were 3 major systems at that time

### Permanent Settlements or Zamindari System (1793)



* Introduced by: **Lord Cornwallis**.
* Planned by: John Shore.
* Region: **Bengal**, **Bihar**, **Odisha**, **Uttar Pradesh**, and **Madhya Pradesh**.
* Zamindars as Owners: The Zamindars (intermediaries) were recognized as the owners of the land. They collected rent from the peasants.
* Revenue Share: The British government took 10/11th of the revenue, and the Zamindar kept 1/11th.
* Sunset Clause: A strict law was introduced requiring Zamindars to deposit the revenue by a specific date before sunset. If they failed to do so, their hereditary zamindari rights were confiscated and auctioned.
* Lord Cornwallis introduced Daroga System replacing Zamindari Thanedars.
* Impact: This system created a class of loyal landlords but led to the exploitation of peasants (Ryots).

### Ryotwari (रैयत) System (1820)



* Introduced by: Thomas **Munro** and Captain Alexander **Read**.
* Region: **South India** (Madras region of Tamilnadu).
* Direct Settlement: There were no Zamindars. The revenue settlement was made directly with the रैयतs (Farmers).
* Ricardian Theory: It was based on the Ricardian Theory of Rent (by economist David Ricardo).
* Ownership: Peasants were given ownership rights as long as they paid taxes.
* Usually there was a 30 year settlement beteen Brits and रैयतs.

### Mahalwari System (1822)



* Introduced by: **Holt Mackenzie** (Concept) and later reformed by **William Bentinck** (1833).
* Region: North-Western Provinces, Central India, **Punjab**, and parts of Uttar Pradesh (Gangetic Valley).
* The revenue was settled with the entire village or group of villages, called a "Mahal".
* The Village Headman (Lambardar) collected revenue from farmers on behalf of the whole village, not a Zamindar.

### Sanyasi Revolt

---

* **1763** ~1800 (Sham)
* It started in Bihar and Bengal.
* Context: Governor-General Warren Hastings faced persistent disturbances from Sanyasis and Fakirs.
* Role of **Warren Hastings**: He took strict military and administrative measures to suppress the Sanyasi and Fakir disturbances.
* Leaders: Bhawani **Pathak**, **Majnu** Shah, and **Debi Chaudhurani** (famous female participant).
* Literary Reference: The revolt is mentioned in **Bankim Chandra Chatterjee’s** famous **novel Anandmath**.

### Paika Revolt

---

* **1817**-25 (TC)
* Armed uprising against the British East India Company in Odisha
* Paikas were farmer / soldiers of Gajapati rulers of Odisha
* Under Brits, Paikas lost their nish-kar jagirs that were given to them for their service
* The removal of King of Khurda, who was also custodian of Jagannath Temple enraged Odia people
* Leader of the rebellion: **Bakshi Jagbandhu Bidyadhar** (Military Commander of the King Khurda)
* Martial law was imposed to crush the rebellion which was successful
* However, some rebels continued to engage in guerrilla warfare

### Santhal Rebellione

---

* Before the Santhals, the Pahariya tribe inhabited the Rajmahal Hills (Jharkhand) and resisted British encroachment.
* Pahariya Revolt (1778): Led by **Raja Jagannath**, they revolted against British land policies that threatened their traditional lifestyle of shifting cultivation and hunting.
* British Strategy: To counter the Pahariya resistance and clear the forests for settled agriculture, the British encouraged the Santhals to settle in this region.
* Damin-i-Koh: The area around Rajmahal Hills was demarcated and **declared as Damin-i-Koh in 1832**. It was officially declared as the land of the Santhals.
* Note: Santhals is the 3rd largest tribe in India.

### Causes of the Revolt of 1857

---

AKA The Sepoy Mutiny

Causes of the Revolt
### Doctrine of Lapse (1848)



* Passed by **Dalhousie**.
* If an Indian ruler died without a male heir, his kingdom would lapse to the British.
* Annexed States Sequence:

1. Satara (1848)
2. Jaitpur & Sambalpur (1849)
3. Udaipur (1852)
4. Jhasi (1853)
5. Nagpur (1854)

### General Service Enlistment Act (1856)

* Passed by Lord **Canning**.
* It mandated that Bengal Army sepoys must serve overseas if required.
* Back then crossing the sea was considered a loss of caste.

### Annexation of Awadh (1856)



* Awadh was captured on the grounds of Misgovernance from Mirza Wajid Ali Shah (the last Nawab of Awadh) which was just an excuse.

### Mangal Pandey



* Due to spread of a rumor that the cartridges for new **Enfield Rifle** were greased with Cow or Pig fat had offended the Hindu and Muslim sentiments.
* Barrackpore Incident: Mangal Pandey (a sepoy in the **34th Bengal Native Infantry**) attacked British officers on 29-03-1857. So, on 08-04-1857, Mangal Pandey was hanged.
* Meerut Incident: on 09-05-1857, lot sepoys were sentensed to 10 years in jail for refusing the cartridges.

The Revolt of 1857 formally began in meerut on **10-05-1857** with a march towards Delhi.

### Aftermath of 1857 revolt

---

### Government of India Act 1858

* The East India Company was abolished, and power was transferred to the British Crown.
* The position of Governor-General was changed to Viceroy. The first Viceroy was Lord Canning.
* The army was reorganized based on the Peel Commission recommendations to reduce the proportion of Indian soldiers.

### Deccan Revolt (?)

---

Deccan Revolt (**1875**) (Cool)

* This revolt was started in Maharashtra (Deccan region).
* Under Limitation Law of 1859 loan bonds were only valid for 3 years, So money lenders used to force farmers to sign new bonds every 3 years, which meant adding the unpaid interest into principal amount.
* Brits used to import most of their cotton from Amarica but due to civil war supply chain was interupted, so Brits promoted cotton to Indian farmers.
* But when the Amarican civil war (1861-65) ended the prices of Indian cotton crashed and the farmers could not pay their loans.
* Farmers started rioting against moneylenders, farmers started burning houses to burn documents that were proof of their loan.
* Brits setup Deccan Riots Commission (1878) to investigate the revolt.
* And the Deccan Agriculturists' Relief Act (1879) was passed to protect farmers.
